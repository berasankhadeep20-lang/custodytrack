# How to Apply — RailTech Portal

**Honest caveat first:** the portal (`railtech.indianrailways.gov.in`) blocks
automated access, so I couldn't inspect its actual submission form fields
directly — everything below is based on the official PIB press release and
policy coverage, not a first-hand look at the form. Treat the proposal doc as
content ready to paste/adapt into whatever fields the real form asks for, and
verify field-by-field once you're actually on the site.

## Steps

**1. Go to the portal.**
`https://railtech.indianrailways.gov.in/`

**2. Register/create an account** as an individual innovator, if the portal
requires it (standard for this type of government portal — expect to need a
verified email and phone number).

**3. Look for how to submit.** Per the official announcement, "innovation
challenges can be initiated by any innovator or departmental user" — meaning
you don't have to wait for Railways to post a matching open problem
statement. If there's a "Submit your own proposal" or "Propose a Challenge"
option distinct from responding to posted challenges, that's the one you
want. If the portal instead only shows a fixed list of open challenges,
check whether any existing one is close enough to submit under (e.g. a
"passenger services" or "digital operations" category) — if truly none
fits, look for a general contact/enquiry route to ask where an
unsolicited proposal like this belongs.

**4. Fill in the proposal using `docs/RAILTECH_PROPOSAL.md`** as your source
content — problem statement, existing-measures differentiation, technical
readiness, pilot plan, cost ask, all already drafted with real numbers.

**5. Attach supporting material.** Link the GitHub repo and the live status
site directly in the submission if there's a "supporting links/documents"
field — a working system with visible commit history and a documented bug
trail is unusually strong evidence for a student proposal at this stage.

**6. Submit, and save/screenshot your confirmation.** Government portals
often send a reference/tracking number — keep it somewhere safe, you'll need
it for any follow-up.

## After submitting

- Realistic timeline: government evaluation committees typically take
  **weeks to a few months** to respond, even under a "simplified single-stage"
  process. Don't read silence in the first few weeks as rejection.
- If you don't hear back in a reasonable window (try ~6-8 weeks), a polite
  follow-up email referencing your tracking number is normal and expected,
  not pushy.
- **In parallel, consider Smart India Hackathon 2026** (registration closes
  September 2026) as a second, complementary channel — a strong SIH showing
  is itself credibility that can support a RailTech follow-up, and it comes
  with structured mentorship a solo submission doesn't get.

## If the portal itself doesn't cooperate

Government portals launched weeks ago sometimes have rough edges. If you hit
a dead end (broken form, unclear category, no response mechanism), a
reasonable fallback is emailing the Ministry of Railways' public
correspondence channel directly, referencing the RailTech Policy launch by
name (26 Feb 2026) and asking how an individual student innovator should
submit — citing the policy's own framing back at them ("anyone with a strong
technological idea should be able to approach the Railways") is a reasonable,
polite way to make sure a real proposal doesn't just get lost.
